# Pipes 5.1.60

## Continuous playback, full playlist queues, and personal discovery

- Playlist and album playback now exposes the complete selected queue immediately instead of preparing only six upcoming songs. Shuffle uses the full queued collection, and **Up next**, Play next, removal, and refill follow the actual playback order.
- Upcoming online songs resolve their audio when loaded, avoiding stream URLs expiring while waiting in a long playlist. Offline copies still take priority. Failed connections refresh and retry without forcing playback over a pause or another app's audio focus.
- Online autoplay and radio add up to 30 related songs per batch and keep refilling, including after a playlist runs out. Newly added tracks remain reachable during shuffle; temporary recommendation failures retry automatically. If a source mix runs out of new recordings, older related songs can return to keep music flowing. Repeat modes retain their chosen behavior.
- Background playback remains in the media service with network wake support and Android audio focus: other apps can stay open, and playback yields when another app takes audio focus.
- Home now starts from recent listening, liked songs and artists, and saved playlist/album tracks, including on a fresh launch. Related-song recommendations carry the musical context of those recordings and respect the recommendation region. Unplayed suggestions do not become taste signals. Recent history stays on your device.


## Automatic Spotify playlist updates

- Imported public Spotify playlists now remember their source and refresh about once a minute while Pipes is open, including when you return to the app. Newly added Spotify songs appear in the Pipes equivalent automatically. **Refresh now** checks immediately.
- Spotify song additions, removals and order are mirrored. Your Pipes playlist name, custom artwork and separately added songs stay saved. Existing song IDs stay stable across reordering to preserve likes and offline downloads. Copies made with **Add all to…** remain independent.
- Saved playlist songs now appear in Search and remain visible while online results load or the connection is unavailable.
- Failed or incomplete refreshes keep the last saved playlist. Old imports need their Spotify link pasted once more because earlier versions did not retain it; a matching Spotify import with its original name reconnects in place.


## Playlist and album downloads, sorting, and offline playback

- Open a saved playlist or custom album and choose **Download all**, or choose **Download album** on an artist release. Downloading an artist release also saves its album in Library for offline browsing. Choose a category once; songs already downloaded or queued are skipped. Preparation shows progress and lets you retry songs that could not be prepared while successful transfers continue.
- Completed downloads now play directly from the original song entry in likes, playlists, albums, queues, and Android Auto. Saved song identity and collection artwork stay intact. Missing files fall back to online playback and can be downloaded again.
- A collection shows how many songs are available offline. Choose **Downloaded only** to browse and play its local songs without attempting unsaved songs.
- Sort collections by saved order, title in either direction, artist, or duration. **Play all** follows the displayed order. Library can sort likes, downloads, playlists, and albums by title. Sort choices survive restarts and saved collection order stays intact.
- Download controls and song rows distinguish queued, saved offline, and unavailable files with status icons. Online songs keep their source's compressed audio without another compression pass; local files and direct-link files keep their original format.

## Learn Pipes

- A seven-lesson tutorial introduces discovery and search, playback, queues and radio, collections, cover artwork, offline music, and settings. It is offered once on first launch after this update, including for existing users.
- Try the navigation tabs and the actual waveform controls silently inside the guide. Lessons explain minimize versus stop, Play next versus Add to queue, playlist saves versus downloads, song-row versus mini-player swipes, and Spotify's role as a metadata source.
- **Try…** opens the relevant screen in the real app. A **Continue tutorial** bar returns you to your lesson. Missing playback is handled with a Search shortcut and a clear prompt to choose a song first.
- Pause with **Save & close**, navigate with **Back** and **Next lesson**, or finish and replay from **Settings → Learn Pipes**. Progress survives restarts; the guide itself works offline.

## Behavior and wording that now match

- Starting a song from Home, Search, Explore, Library, a collection, an artist release, or a local file consistently opens the full player. Minimize returns to where you started. Starting radio from the queue preserves that browsing origin instead of returning to an empty queue after stopping.
- Swiping a song left consistently removes it from the upcoming queue, including inside a collection. Use the visible remove button or bulk **Remove** to edit the collection; a queue gesture no longer deletes a saved song entry.
- The settings and local-file buttons are available in Library's top bar. Settings describes the current waveform player rather than the retired vinyl control.
- Library counts playlists and custom albums separately. **Download link** clearly identifies the manual link form; use Search for YouTube and Spotify links, then a song's Download action for offline audio.
- Artwork and deletion notices name collections and albums accurately. The README now includes the tutorial, current controls, and matching download instructions.

## Your playlist and album artwork

- Open a playlist or custom album and choose **Choose artwork** to pick an image or animated GIF from your device. Your cover appears in the playlist, Library, and the Add to… picker.
- Covers are copied into app storage, so moving or deleting the original file does not remove your artwork. GIFs keep their animation.
- **Change artwork** replaces a cover; **Use default artwork** restores the first song’s cover or the playlist icon. Playlist edits, renaming, and app restarts preserve your selection.

## Kirby inhale animation

Adding songs to a playlist or custom album now plays the supplied Kirby inhale GIF. Kirby faces left while the selected covers fly in from the left and shrink into his mouth after a successful save.

## Downloads & your collections

- Download the playing song from the full player, mini-player, or song menu. Choose an existing download category; the best available audio is saved to the offline library. Local files and active/completed downloads are recognized. Failed transfers can be retried.
- Choose your own playlist or custom album every time you add songs. The player and liked songs no longer silently create a Commute Mix. Existing saved collections stay available.
- Create, rename, change collection type, or delete playlists and custom albums. Remove individual songs or a bulk selection. Deleting a collection leaves downloaded files and other collections intact.
- Create a collection directly while adding music without losing the selected songs. Add one song, an entire artist release, or all songs from another playlist or album; duplicates are skipped and song order is preserved. Collection copies are independent snapshots.
- Hold a song to select it, tap more songs, then choose **Add to…**. Use **Select all**, **Remove** inside a collection, or **Cancel**. The three-dot menu retains queue, radio, like and download actions.
- A left-facing animated Kirby in the lower-right corner inhales shrinking cover artwork after a successful addition, including bulk saves.

Song downloads depend on the source exposing a progressive audio stream. They keep the available compressed format; they do not convert streams to lossless audio.

A monochrome refresh with charcoal surfaces, neutral waveform seeking and a silver play control. Color appears in selected navigation, shuffle, repeat and liked states.

The full player opens into the artwork atmosphere without a boxed transport panel. Larger responsive artwork, a shared spacing grid, subtle drifting stars and tactile playback actions bring the controls together. Home uses a smaller header and a wider featured cover to show more music.

Discovery refreshes when saved artist preferences change and prioritizes those artists over generic mood searches. Suggestions no longer recursively seed personalized artist browsing. With no preferences, a varied mood feed helps you begin; playing music and liking artists tune subsequent recommendations.

The waveform retains drag previews and release-to-seek behavior, with a light haptic on commit. Playback, queue, radio, playlists and local audio remain available.

## Live music & swipe controls

- Swipe the mini-player song row left for next or right for previous, with spring feedback and a light haptic. The seek bar keeps its own drag gesture.
- Browse a swipeable artwork carousel on Home. Your current track leads with live audio motion; tap to open the full player or play a discovery card.
- The main waveform now responds to actual decoded audio amplitude. Pause, buffering and scrubbing restore the original seek pattern. No microphone permission or audio recording is needed.
- Audio analysis passes playback samples through unchanged; visual updates stop while the app is in the background.

## Playback startup hotfix

Fixes “Unexpected runtime error” when starting music. The audio visualizer now ignores Media3’s empty polling buffers instead of attempting to copy a buffer onto itself. Regression coverage includes empty startup polls, audio pass-through, end-of-stream draining and sample-rate changes.

## Spectrum waveform refinement

The visualizer retains the original sculpted waveform at all times. Real bass, midrange and treble frequencies lift its bars with fast attacks and a slower falloff, subtle monochrome bloom and additional vertical breathing room. Silence, pause and scrubbing restore the original silhouette. Stereo FFT analysis replaces the volume-history strip, and spectrum rendering updates without rebuilding the cached bar geometry on every audio frame.

## iPhone & iPad 5.1.55

The native iOS app now adapts the 5.1 experience: four-tab browsing, cached mood/genre Explore, artwork-atmosphere player with waveform seeking, persistent queue with Play next, shuffle/repeat, song radio, editable playlists/custom albums, bulk selection, copied image/GIF covers, and seven resumable Learn Pipes lessons. It adds source-link search and direct HTTPS audio downloads into category playlists, while retaining existing iOS likes/imports and local background playback/CarPlay. The Apple workflow runs library regressions and a simulator tutorial/collection/persistence test before publishing an unsigned IPA.

iOS requires sideload signing or Xcode installation. Online YouTube playback requires the visible embedded player and Pipes open; Spotify playlist imports, discographies, YouTube audio extraction, Instagram downloads, the decoded spectrum and Kirby animation remain Android-only. See [the iOS guide](../../ios/README.md) for installation and feature differences.
